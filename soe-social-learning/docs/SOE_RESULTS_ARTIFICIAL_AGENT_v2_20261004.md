# Results draft — Artificial agents, biological computation and causal tests

## Result 2 — A mouse-derived SLM is sufficient to generate the organization of social learning

The behavioral analyses above defined social observational eating as a sequential information-sampling problem rather than as a static tendency to approach or feed. Social information had positive decision value in 22/27 learner animals (median +0.00365 nats per decision; rank-biserial r=0.725; P=0.000268), but this value was strongly state dependent. Social information became less decision-relevant later in learning (late versus early, rank-biserial r=-0.619; P=0.00194) and was more valuable when the observer was farther from its own spout, including 24/27 animals in the no-demonstrator-feeding condition (rank-biserial r=0.905; P=1.88e-6). Thus the core behavioral problem is not simply whether the animal observes another mouse, but when social evidence is useful enough to change the learner's policy.

At each opportunity, the observer must decide whether to sample the demonstrator, assign outcome-specific credit to the consequence of that observation, and carry the resulting social-reward state forward to guide later behavior. We therefore asked whether the SLM inferred from real mice was sufficient to generate this organization when placed back into a social-foraging environment.

We constructed an Artificial SLM Agent whose policy and update rules were fit only from training animals. In held-out animals, the agent generated its own Observe/No-observe actions. A frozen environment model then generated Active, Passive, Unrewarded or None outcomes conditional on the generated action and current social state; these generated outcomes recursively updated SLM memory, contingency-specific belief, value and slow prior. Thus held-out mouse outcomes were not replayed.

Across five held-animal folds, the closed-loop SLM reproduced the broad structure of real learning trajectories (real-versus-generated trajectory r=0.779). This was similar to but not uniformly better than simpler mechanistic agents: Q-learning achieved a slightly lower trajectory RMSE (0.1665 versus 0.1683 for Full SLM). The stronger distinction emerged at the level of local social strategy. The outcome-conditioned behavioral kernel was reproduced more faithfully by Full SLM (r=0.312; RMSE=0.0826) than by baseline (r=0.274; RMSE=0.0899), Q-allreward (r=0.273; RMSE=0.0867), or outcome-belief alone (r=0.246; RMSE=0.0881). Full SLM also showed the highest kernel-sign agreement (0.621). These results indicate that the main generative advantage of SLM lies not in uniformly minimizing every global prediction error, but in preserving the organization of local social-learning strategies.

We next asked whether the artificial agent reproduced movement through the same behavioral state space used to describe real mice. Pre-action states were projected into the frozen v85 12-motif atlas. The projection was independently re-audited on the current ANM4 D139 viewer: all 60 audited frames retained the same motif identity under the current software environment. Held-animal next-motif prediction improved when the observer's own action was added to the current motif (39/40 animals improved; rank-biserial r=0.959; one-sided P=1.88e-10), and improved in all 40 animals when social outcome was added as well (rank-biserial r=1.0; P=9.09e-13). Adding day identity did not improve the transition model. We therefore froze the minimal motif environment as P(m[t+1] | m[t], action, outcome).

When Q, outcome-belief and Full SLM agents were placed in the same frozen outcome and motif environments, Full SLM generated modestly more realistic motif occupancy than the simpler agents (Jensen-Shannon divergence: Full SLM 0.0520, Q 0.0537, belief 0.0542). Full SLM was better than Q in 24/40 animals (one-sided P=0.0257) and better than belief in 25/40 animals (P=0.0130). Transition-edge distributions showed the same directional advantage, although full transition-matrix correlation was essentially identical for Full SLM and Q (0.733 versus 0.734). Thus SLM improves behavioral-state organization without trivially dominating every state-transition metric.

To remove the remaining dependence on held-out observer-state replay, we next constructed an autonomous transition-exemplar world in which generated behavioral state feeds back into the next policy, feature state and outcome process. Because this model is stochastic, we quantified posterior-predictive behavior by averaging ten autonomous rollouts per held-out animal. Without refitting the model, the ten-rollout posterior-predictive mean reproduced the broad learner trajectory (r=0.638), local social-strategy kernel (r=0.444), and motif occupancy (mean occupancy r=0.864; JS divergence=0.0390). A prespecified replication-count audit confirmed that increasing the number of stochastic rollouts primarily reduced Monte-Carlo error rather than changing the model: the same frozen control increased from trajectory r=0.400 for one rollout to 0.495, 0.532, 0.578 and 0.622 after averaging 2, 3, 5 and 10 rollouts across the full 40-animal cohort, with RMSE decreasing monotonically. Consistent with residual slow individual variation, a Day-1-derived slow state improved confirmatory D2+ trajectory correlation from 0.480 to 0.508 and reduced RMSE from 0.2311 to 0.2258 (20/32 animals improved; rank-biserial r=0.468; one-sided P=0.011), without materially improving local kernel or motif occupancy.

Together, these results support a specific biological interpretation. The Artificial SLM Agent is not a complete virtual mouse and is not intended to simulate all motor and feeding behavior. It is a generative model of the social-information sampling controller embedded within a foraging context: when to observe, how outcome-specific social information updates learned value, and how those updates reorganize later behavioral state.

## Result 3 — A generic recurrent agent independently converges on SLM-like policy coordinates

A mouse-derived generative model can establish sufficiency, but it does not show that SLM-like variables represent a natural solution to the task. We therefore trained a separate generic recurrent actor-critic agent in the same empirical 12-motif social world without giving it SLM latent variables.

The recurrent agent chose between Observe and No-observe. Its environment was derived from the empirical SOE event taxonomy (Active Reward, Passive Reward and Unrewarded event families), but its optimization target was deliberately treated as an artificial task definition rather than as a redefinition of the mouse phenotype. We evaluated two reward ontologies, Active-only social reward and Active+Passive food reward, while applying an observation cost to prevent indiscriminate sampling. Reward and cost were calibrated on training animals only; Visual Block and JAWS data were not used to train or select the intact agent. Canonical SRI and the empirically defined SOE outcomes remained the biological reference quantities.

The recurrent agent learned stable task policies and reproduced substantial behavioral-state structure. Across folds and seeds, motif occupancy correlation with held-out learners was approximately 0.75 and transition correlation approximately 0.53. The crucial question was whether the learned recurrent policy could be described by the same variables inferred from mice. Adding explicit SLM coordinates — multiscale memory and source-specific belief variables — to current motif improved held-out policy prediction in every tested fold x seed x reward run: Brier score, negative log likelihood and AUC each improved in 30/30 runs.

This convergence provides a second and conceptually independent role for SLM. The mouse-derived Artificial SLM Agent asks whether the inferred computation is sufficient to regenerate behavior. The generic recurrent agent asks whether a flexible learner, trained to solve the same social-information acquisition problem without privileged access to SLM variables, independently develops a policy that is compactly described by those variables. The result does not imply that the recurrent network implements the exact SLM equations; rather, it identifies SLM coordinates as a low-dimensional description of a policy that can emerge in a more flexible learning system.

## Result 4 — Artificial SLM regenerates computational variables independently represented in VTA

We next asked whether the generative model reproduced not only surface behavior but also the computational geometry inferred from real mice. To avoid circularity, we did not generate a synthetic dopamine trace from fitted neural coefficients. Instead, the same five-fold SLM contract was replayed separately on held-out real-mouse trajectories and on autonomous Artificial SLM trajectories, yielding native policy, action-prediction error (APE), reward-prediction error (RPE), and contingency-specific belief-update variables on both sides.

Across animals, real and artificial trajectories showed strong correspondence in mean sampling policy (Spearman rho=0.789, P=1.50e-9), mean RPE (rho=0.771, P=5.98e-9), mean absolute Active-belief update (rho=0.734, P=7.00e-8), and mean absolute APE (rho=0.497, P=0.00112). Outcome-specific computational geometry was even more strongly preserved: across Active, Passive and Unrewarded outcomes, the median within-animal profile correlation was approximately 1.00 for RPE, 0.994 for Active-belief update and 0.899 for APE. Day-by-day organization was also retained, with positive real-artificial policy correlations in 87.5% of animals.

These artificial-agent variables were generated without neural data. Independently, real VTA dopamine adjudicated the same computations in biological time. Early observation-period VTA activity tracked SLM sampling policy (rank-biserial r=0.810), middle-period activity tracked learner-linked APE (rho=0.667), and post-outcome activity tracked reward/update computations (rank-biserial r=0.867). Flexible behavioral alternatives did not reproduce the same three-stage temporal sequence.

The biological claim is therefore not that the artificial agent simulates dopamine. Rather, the Artificial SLM Agent regenerates computational variables whose temporally distinct expression is independently observed in VTA. This establishes a bridge from a generative solution to the social-information problem to a biological teaching system.

## Result 5 — Information availability and Active-belief updating are separable causal operations

The intact Artificial SLM separates two operations that can be perturbed independently: whether current social evidence is available to guide sampling and whether a social outcome updates the learned contingency-specific state. We therefore applied paired computational lesions to the frozen autonomous world, using the same fitted model and paired random streams across control and lesion conditions. Ten autonomous rollouts were averaged within each animal before inference, and the animal remained the independent statistical unit.

We first modeled Visual Block as a sensory-information lesion: current demonstrator and explicitly social state variables were removed from the sampling policy while the physical environment and the internal learning rules remained intact. This manipulation preserved gross observation (0.4463 in control versus 0.4692 after sensory masking; rank-biserial r=0.407, two-sided P=0.0655), closely matching the real Visual Block experiment in which SRI collapsed while observation frequency, duration and occupancy showed no systematic reduction. Despite preserved observation, sensory masking reduced the Active contingency from 0.0940 to 0.0856 in all 27 learner animals (rank-biserial r=-1.0, P=1.49e-8), and reduced the mean Active social belief from 0.0450 to 0.0376 (rank-biserial r=-0.767, P=0.000209). The magnitude of the direct Active-belief update itself was unchanged (rank-biserial r=0.254, P=0.258), as was Q-update magnitude (rank-biserial r=0.011, P=0.972). Thus removing social sensory information did not damage the learning rule; it changed the information and contingencies sampled by the intact rule.

This sensory lesion also reduced the fidelity of the generated learning trajectory to intact real-mouse behavior: per-animal trajectory correlation decreased by 0.0337 on average (rank-biserial r=-0.619, P=0.00388), whereas the local outcome-conditioned strategy kernel did not reliably change (rank-biserial r=-0.122, P=0.594). Stronger computational lesions were qualitatively less consistent with the biological Visual Block phenotype. Combining sensory masking with complete blockade of contingency-belief updating reduced observation from 0.446 to 0.321 in all 27 animals; blocking belief, Q, slow-state and feature-credit channels reduced it to 0.345. These over-lesions also produced substantially larger degradation of trajectory, kernel and motif fidelity. Within the current artificial world, loss of effective current social information is therefore a closer computational analogue of Visual Block than globally abolishing social-outcome learning.

We next modeled JAWS by reducing only the learning efficacy of Active outcomes in the contingency-specific belief update. A fixed-event counterfactual, in which the validated JAWS event sequence was held constant, first established direct mechanistic selectivity: attenuating Active-outcome learning shifted the Active-belief coordinate downward without changing the event sequence. We then placed the same selective lesion inside the autonomous agent so that its consequences could propagate through future actions, outcomes and behavioral states.

Reducing Active-outcome belief learning efficacy by 50% decreased the mean magnitude of the direct Active-belief update from 0.003308 to 0.002577, a 22.1% reduction, in all 27 learner animals (rank-biserial r=-1.0, P=1.49e-8). By contrast, the mean magnitude of the per-event Q update changed from 0.001835 to 0.001825, only 0.55%; thus the fractional attenuation of the targeted belief update was approximately 40-fold larger than that of the Q update. This scale comparison is important because the very small Q change was directionally consistent across animals and therefore also produced a large signed-rank effect despite its negligible absolute magnitude.

In the closed loop, JAWS50 reduced mean Active belief from 0.0450 to 0.0413 (rank-biserial r=-0.587, P=0.00645), and the Active-minus-Passive belief contrast decreased in 24/27 learner animals (rank-biserial r=-0.889, P=6.66e-6). Observation was modestly reduced from 0.4463 to 0.4287. Crucially, the generated Active contingency was essentially preserved (0.0940 versus 0.0946; two-sided P=0.052). Global learning-trajectory correlation was also unchanged (mean paired change -0.00008; rank-biserial r=-0.016, P=0.953), whereas local strategy-kernel correlation decreased (mean change -0.0123; rank-biserial r=-0.582, P=0.0070). Thus a selective Active-belief teaching lesion reorganized local social strategy without collapsing the global behavioral trajectory or the physical social contingency.

Together, the two artificial lesions form a computational double dissociation. Removing current social information reduced the Active contingency and learned Active belief while leaving the Active-belief update rule intact. Selectively weakening Active-belief teaching strongly reduced the update itself while leaving the Active contingency largely intact. This mirrors the experimental distinction between Visual Block, which preserves gross observation but disrupts the learned SOE phenotype, and JAWS, which disrupts the Active social-belief coordinate while sparing generic choice persistence. The artificial lesions do not imply that the biological manipulations act exclusively on a single mathematical variable; rather, they show that the intact SLM contains separable information-access and state-updating operations whose perturbation generates distinct experimentally observed signatures. Because the current event-level agent freely chooses Observe/No-observe but does not yet generate a native future feeding action, this causal closure applies to information sampling, credit assignment and local strategy rather than to a synthetic reconstruction of canonical SRI itself.
